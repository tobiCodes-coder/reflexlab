What Is a Good Typing Speed?

ReflexLab Guides · Updated 2026

Quick answer: For most people, 40–60 WPM is a solid everyday typing speed. Above 60 WPM is fast, 80–100 WPM is very fast, and 100+ WPM is exceptional. Accuracy matters just as much as speed.

Typing speed is one of the most useful measures of keyboard performance. Whether you are writing documents, programming, chatting, studying, gaming, or working online, being able to type efficiently can save time and make everyday computer tasks easier.

But what actually counts as a good typing speed?

For most people, a typing speed of around 40 to 60 words per minute (WPM) is a solid everyday range. Speeds above 60 WPM are generally considered fast, while reaching 80 to 100+ WPM is an impressive level of typing performance.

However, typing speed is only part of the picture. Accuracy matters just as much as speed, and a fast typist who makes frequent mistakes may be less efficient than someone who types slightly slower with high accuracy.

What Does WPM Mean?

WPM stands for Words Per Minute.

It measures approximately how many words you can type in one minute.

Typing tests generally use a standardized word length when calculating WPM. A common convention treats five characters, including spaces, as one word.

For example, if you type 300 characters in one minute:

300 ÷ 5 = 60 WPM

This allows typing speeds to be compared even when people type words of different lengths.

What Is Considered a Good Typing Speed?

There is no single speed that is officially defined as "good." It depends on your age, experience, purpose, and accuracy.

A useful general reference is:

Typing SpeedGeneral Level
Under 20 WPMBeginner
20–30 WPMDeveloping
30–40 WPMBasic
40–60 WPMGood
60–80 WPMFast
80–100 WPMVery fast
100+ WPMExceptional

These categories are approximate rather than strict standards.

Someone who types 45 WPM with excellent accuracy may be more effective in real-world work than someone who reaches 70 WPM while making frequent mistakes.

Is 40 WPM Good?

Yes.

40 WPM is a respectable everyday typing speed, especially for someone who does not type regularly.

At this speed, you can handle many common tasks such as:

  • Writing messages
  • Taking notes
  • Completing schoolwork
  • Writing documents
  • Browsing the web
  • Entering information into forms

For general computer use, 40 WPM provides a useful foundation.

Is 50 WPM Good?

50 WPM is a good practical typing speed.

At 50 WPM, you can produce text at a comfortable pace without spending excessive time looking for individual keys.

For everyday office work, schoolwork, communication, and general computer use, this is a strong target.

If you can maintain 50 WPM while keeping your accuracy high, you already have an efficient typing skill.

Is 60 WPM Fast?

Yes.

60 WPM is generally considered fast for everyday typing.

At this speed, you can write a significant amount of text in a relatively short period.

For many people, reaching 60 WPM is a worthwhile goal because it provides a noticeable improvement over slower typing without requiring extremely advanced technique.

Is 80 WPM Good?

80 WPM is a very fast typing speed.

Someone who can consistently type around 80 WPM with good accuracy has developed strong keyboard familiarity and coordination.

At this level, typing can become less of a bottleneck when writing long documents, coding, taking notes, or communicating online.

Is 100 WPM Fast?

Definitely.

100 WPM is an exceptional typing speed for general users.

Maintaining 100 WPM requires strong keyboard familiarity, efficient finger movement, good rhythm, and excellent coordination.

However, reaching 100 WPM is not necessary for most everyday tasks. A person who types 60 WPM accurately can already work very efficiently.

Average Typing Speed

Average typing speed varies depending on the population being measured and the testing method.

For general adult computer users, around 40 WPM is often used as a broad reference point for average typing ability.

However, averages can change significantly depending on factors such as:

  • Age
  • Keyboard experience
  • Occupation
  • Language
  • Typing method
  • Testing conditions
  • Accuracy requirements

Someone who spends several hours each day typing is naturally likely to perform differently from someone who rarely uses a keyboard.

Because of these differences, there is no single number that perfectly represents the typing speed of everyone.

Typing Speed vs. Typing Accuracy

Typing speed and accuracy should always be considered together.

Imagine two typists:

TypistSpeedAccuracy
Typist A75 WPM88%
Typist B60 WPM99%

Although Typist A has the higher raw WPM, Typist B may produce usable text more efficiently because far fewer corrections are required.

This is why a good typing test should report both speed and accuracy.

Why Accuracy Matters

Every mistake can create additional work.

If you type a sentence quickly but need to stop repeatedly to correct errors, some of the time saved by typing faster disappears.

For practical typing, a useful goal is often:

Increase speed while maintaining high accuracy.

Speed without control is just fast keyboard noise.

What Is a Good Typing Accuracy?

A typing accuracy of 95% or higher is generally a useful target for most typing practice.

An accuracy of 98–100% is excellent.

For competitive typing tests, some people deliberately push their speed even if it causes additional errors. For everyday work, however, consistently high accuracy is usually more valuable.

A practical combination might be:

60 WPM + 98% accuracy

rather than:

80 WPM + 85% accuracy

The better choice depends on the task, but accuracy becomes increasingly important when the text must be correct.

What Affects Typing Speed?

Many factors influence how quickly someone can type.

Typing Technique

People who use a structured touch-typing technique can often type efficiently without constantly looking at the keyboard.

Touch typing involves learning the positions of keys so that your fingers can move largely from memory.

However, you do not have to use textbook touch typing to become fast. Different people develop different keyboard habits.

Keyboard Familiarity

Using the same keyboard regularly can improve performance.

You become familiar with:

  • Key positions
  • Key spacing
  • Key resistance
  • Keyboard layout
  • Your preferred hand position

Switching between very different keyboards can temporarily affect your speed.

Practice

Typing is a skill that can improve through deliberate practice.

Regular practice can help you develop:

  • Muscle memory
  • Finger coordination
  • Accuracy
  • Rhythm
  • Familiarity with common words and key combinations

The goal should not be to blindly chase higher WPM. Practicing accurate typing at a controlled speed usually provides a stronger foundation.

Language

Typing speed depends heavily on the language being tested.

Different languages have different word lengths, character frequencies, punctuation patterns, and keyboard requirements.

A WPM score in English should therefore not automatically be treated as directly equivalent to a typing score in another language.

Keyboard Layout

QWERTY is widely used, but other keyboard layouts exist.

Different layouts place letters in different positions and can affect typing performance. Someone highly experienced with one layout may initially type much more slowly when switching to another.

What Is a Good Typing Speed for Students?

For students, a speed around 40–60 WPM can be a useful practical target.

Students often use typing for:

  • Assignments
  • Research
  • Note-taking
  • Online learning
  • Emails and messages
  • Projects

A faster typing speed can make these tasks more efficient, but accuracy should remain a priority.

There is also no need for every student to reach 100 WPM. The ideal target is the speed that allows you to complete your work comfortably and accurately.

What Is a Good Typing Speed for Programming?

Programming is slightly different from ordinary typing.

A programmer does not spend every second typing text. Programming involves reading code, thinking about problems, navigating files, using shortcuts, debugging, and deciding what to write.

Because of this, extremely high WPM is not required to become a good programmer.

A typing speed of 50–70 WPM with strong accuracy is already more than enough for many coding tasks.

For programmers, keyboard familiarity and efficient shortcuts can often provide more practical benefits than chasing an extremely high typing speed.

Knowing shortcuts for navigation, editing, selecting text, duplicating lines, and moving around code can save significant time.

What Is a Good Typing Speed for Office Work?

For general office work, 40–60 WPM is a useful range.

People who regularly handle large amounts of text may benefit from higher speeds, particularly in jobs involving:

  • Data entry
  • Transcription
  • Customer support
  • Documentation
  • Content writing
  • Administrative work

In these environments, accuracy and consistency can be just as important as raw speed.

What Is a Good Typing Speed for Gaming?

Typing speed is not a major factor in most games, but it can be useful in games that involve frequent text communication.

A faster typist can communicate with teammates more quickly without spending as much time looking at the keyboard.

However, gaming performance itself depends on many other skills, so typing speed should not be treated as a general measure of gaming ability.

How Long Does It Take to Improve Typing Speed?

There is no fixed timeline.

Improvement depends on your current skill level, practice frequency, technique, and consistency.

Someone starting at 20 WPM may see noticeable improvement relatively quickly with regular practice, while moving from 70 WPM to 90 WPM may require much more deliberate training.

Progress also tends to become less dramatic as speeds get higher.

For example, improving from:

25 → 40 WPM

may be easier than improving from:

80 → 100 WPM

The higher your speed becomes, the more precision and consistency matter.

How to Improve Your Typing Speed

If you want to increase your WPM, focus on building accuracy first.

1. Learn Key Positions

Become familiar with where the keys are so you do not need to search for them visually.

2. Practice Regularly

Short, consistent practice sessions can be more useful than occasional long sessions.

3. Prioritize Accuracy

Try to make fewer mistakes before pushing your speed higher.

4. Reduce Looking at the Keyboard

Gradually train yourself to rely more on keyboard memory.

5. Practice Common Words

Common words and letter combinations appear repeatedly in everyday writing. Becoming comfortable with them can improve typing flow.

6. Use Consistent Test Conditions

If you are tracking your progress, use the same keyboard, language, test duration, and similar conditions whenever possible.

7. Track Your Average

Your personal best is fun, but your average score gives you a better idea of your normal performance.

Why Your WPM Can Change Between Tests

It is completely normal for typing scores to fluctuate.

For example:

52 WPM → 58 WPM → 55 WPM → 61 WPM → 57 WPM

These differences do not necessarily represent major changes in your actual typing ability.

Your performance can be affected by:

  • Concentration
  • Fatigue
  • Familiarity with the text
  • Keyboard position
  • Typing rhythm
  • Mistakes
  • Distractions

If you want to measure improvement, look at your performance over many tests rather than focusing on one unusually high or low score.

Gross WPM vs. Net WPM

Some typing tests distinguish between gross WPM and net WPM.

Gross WPM represents your raw typing speed before accounting fully for mistakes.

Net WPM attempts to account for errors and therefore gives a better indication of usable typing performance.

For example, someone may have a high gross WPM but a lower net WPM because of frequent mistakes.

When comparing results between different typing tests, check how each website calculates its score. Two tests can report different WPM values from the same performance if their scoring systems differ.

Does a Higher WPM Always Mean Better Typing?

No.

Typing is a balance between:

Speed + Accuracy + Consistency

A person who can type 60 WPM with 99% accuracy may be more effective than someone who reaches 90 WPM but frequently makes errors.

The best typing speed is therefore not necessarily your fastest possible speed. It is the fastest speed at which you can still produce reliable, accurate text.

What Is a Good Typing Speed? The Bottom Line

For general computer use, 40–60 WPM is a good practical typing speed.

A speed of 60–80 WPM is fast, while 80–100 WPM is very fast and 100+ WPM is exceptional.

But WPM should never be considered by itself.

Accuracy, consistency, typing technique, keyboard familiarity, language, and the purpose of typing all influence what counts as good performance.

If you are currently below your target, do not worry about chasing someone else's score. Set a realistic personal goal and track your progress over time.

Whether your goal is 40 WPM, 60 WPM, 80 WPM, or 100+ WPM, the most useful improvement is the one that makes typing feel easier, more accurate, and more natural.
